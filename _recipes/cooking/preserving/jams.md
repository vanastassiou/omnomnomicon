_Makes about 1 litre, roughly 3.5 to 4 half-pint (250 mL) jars depending on evaporation._

### Equipment

- A large, broad, non-reactive pot 3 to 4 times the volume of the uncooked mixture, to allow for expansion and foaming.
- A water bath canner: a pot tall enough to hold a wire rack, the jars, and at least 3 inches of clearance above them.
- Mason jars, new snap lids, and ring bands.
- Jar lifter tongs.
- A magnet for retrieving hot snap lids from the water.

### Method

1. Combine the blueberries, apple stock, sugar, and lemon juice (or 1.25 mL citric acid) in the pot and let sit until the sugar fully dissolves.
2. Bring to a rolling boil, stirring frequently to prevent scorching, and cook until the jam gels at 220°F (104°C).
3. Ladle the hot jam into hot, sterilized jars, leaving 12 mm (1/2 inch) of headspace, then wipe the rims.
4. Seal with snap lids that have sat in hot water for 5 minutes to soften the seals.
5. Process the jars in a boiling water bath for 10 minutes.

### Notes

- Apple stock adds pectin and brightens the flavour, but the extra liquid means a longer boil to reach a set.
- Lemon juice helps ensure a good set and shelf stability.
