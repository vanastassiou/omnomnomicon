_Makes one 9x5 pan loaf, about 10 slices._

At 40% total rye, this loaf carries a denser, moister crumb than pure wheat sourdough. Rye ferments faster than wheat, so watch the dough rather than the clock.

1. Autolyse: mix the flours and vital wheat gluten dry, then add the water. Rest 30 to 45 minutes.
2. Add the starter, salt, and honey (if using). Pinch and fold to incorporate, about 3 to 4 minutes.
3. Bulk ferment 4 to 6 hours at about 75°F (24°C). Perform 3 to 4 sets of stretch-and-folds in the first 2 hours, then leave undisturbed.
4. Pre-shape gently and bench rest 15 minutes.
5. Shape into a log and place seam-side down in a greased or parchment-lined 9x5 pan.
6. Cover and refrigerate 10 to 14 hours.
7. Remove from the fridge and proof until the dough crowns about 1 inch above the pan rim.
8. Preheat the oven to 450°F (230°C). Bake 15 minutes, then reduce to 400°F (205°C) and bake another 30 to 35 minutes, tenting with foil if the top darkens too quickly. The internal temperature should reach about 205°F (96°C).
9. Rest in the pan 10 minutes, then turn out onto a wire rack. Allow at least 1 hour before slicing; rye crumb continues setting as it cools.

### Notes

- If adding honey, add 3% of the flour weight (about 17 g) for a subtle sweetness that rounds out the rye bitterness, or 5% (about 28 g) for a noticeable honey flavour and more browning. Reduce the water by roughly half the honey weight to keep the hydration balanced.
- Start the vital wheat gluten at 3% (17 g) for better rise and sliceability. Increase to 5% (about 28 g) only if the loaf is too dense or collapses after cooling. Mix it in dry with the flours before adding water.
- Grease the pan well or line it with parchment; rye sticks aggressively.
- Dark rye absorbs more water. If the dough feels tight during mixing, the full 400 mL is appropriate; if it feels slack, hold back 15 to 20 mL.
- For a more open crumb, shift the flour ratios to 70/15/15 (all-purpose/light rye/dark rye). For a more rye-forward loaf, push to 50/25/25 or 40/30/30, though shaping becomes progressively harder.
