_Makes about 2 pounds (900 g)._

### Prepare the beans

1. Rinse the beans and cover with enough water to submerge them about 4 inches deep. Soak 8 to 24 hours, changing the water after 12 hours.
2. Drain the beans, re-cover with water, and massage to remove the hulls. Alternatively, roughly chop the beans in a food processor.
3. Steam whole beans 10 minutes in a pressure cooker. For chopped beans, boil on the stovetop about 45 minutes, until al dente.
4. Drain the beans, transfer to a casserole pan, and add the vinegar.
5. Fold and stir the beans to release steam and remove surface moisture.
6. Add the starter and stir until well incorporated.

### Incubate

1. Transfer the beans to your incubation containers.
2. Incubate at 85°F to 90°F (29°C to 32°C) for about 12 hours. The mould dies if held at 105°F (41°C) for an hour or more.
3. Check the internal temperature and adjust the heat down if it is climbing.
4. At 18 to 24 hours, turn off the heat to the dehydrator or proofing box and let the beans ferment another 6 to 12 hours. If using a water bath, change nothing. The tempeh is done when it forms a firm white cake.
5. To pasteurize, preheat the oven to 180°F (82°C) and bake 30 minutes.

### Notes

- Alternative substrates:
  - Whole grains: soak and cook until al dente, then mix with legumes or pulses to make a complete protein. Millet should be toasted and cooked like rice on the stovetop.
  - Legumes: soak and steam until al dente (refer to an Instant Pot cooking chart); chop or grind coarsely if large, like peanuts. Chickpeas are easiest as chana dal, cooked al dente.
  - Nuts: use no more than 25% nuts by weight and chop them before cooking.
  - Seeds: use 2 to 3 Tbsp per cup of beans or grains and chop before cooking.
  - Pseudograins such as quinoa: soak and cook al dente.
- Acceptable incubation containers:
  - A Ziploc bag or retired plastic food storage container with tiny holes poked in it.
  - Banana leaves, the original method.
  - Silicone soap moulds, covered with perforated plastic wrap.
- Unpasteurized tempeh keeps 2 to 3 days in the refrigerator; pasteurized keeps at least 5 days, up to 2 weeks.
- If the tempeh develops black spots, it is getting overripe: still good for some recipes, but it can quickly turn inedibly strong.
